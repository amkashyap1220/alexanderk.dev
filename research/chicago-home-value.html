How old the data is: pulled Oct 21, 2026. Prices: Redfin, Jun 2025 to May 2026. Rent, home age and the Census commute: ACS 2020–2024. Crime: FBI, 2025 (2024 where 2025 is incomplete). Schools: 2024–25 tests (IL), 2025–26 (IN). Walkability: EPA, 2021. Drive times: OpenStreetMap, Oct 2026. Train stations: Metra, CTA and South Shore Line, Oct 2026.

How it works

  • Give each factor a target and a weight.
  • A zip scores 100 when it hits every target, and less the further off it is. Beating a target for schools, safety, price or train station counts as a hit.
  • With two people, "best for both" uses the lower of your two scores.
  • Click a place in the list to see what's for sale or rent there.

The data

  • Schools: district test scores, as a percentile within the state (Illinois Report Card, Indiana DOE).
  • Safety: crimes per 1,000 residents a year (FBI).
  • Commute: the Census average, or the drive time to where you work (OpenStreetMap, no traffic).
  • Train station: straight-line miles to the nearest Metra, CTA 'L' or South Shore Line station, averaged over where people in the zip live (Census 2020 population centers).
  • Walkability: EPA index, 1 to 20.
  • Home age and rent: Census ACS 2020–2024. Rent is per person: the median rent divided by the average bedrooms of rented homes, so a $2,500 two-bedroom is $1,250 each.
  • Price: median sale price, June 2025 to May 2026 (Redfin).

Covers the 13-county Chicago metro. Race and ethnicity aren't used. A zip averages very different streets, so treat a pick as a place worth a visit. Method and code are on GitHub.
